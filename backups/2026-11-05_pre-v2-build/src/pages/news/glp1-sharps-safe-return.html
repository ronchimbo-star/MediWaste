Objective

Keep used injection pen needles out of household waste and recycling streams.

The issue

Used injection sharps need a safer, simpler route

More residents are self-administering GLP-1 weight-loss and diabetes medicines. Used pen needles must be disposed of in approved sharps containers — not household bins or recycling.

Needle-stick risk

Discarded needles create a serious injury risk for residents, recycling-centre staff, waste operatives and street-cleaning teams.

Recycling contamination

Sharps entering recycling or general waste can disrupt operations and create avoidable safety incidents.

Inconvenient disposal

Residents may have to wait for a household collection or travel further than necessary to find a suitable return point.

Benefits

Practical benefits for councils, residents and partners

The pilot is designed to be low-disruption, measurable and easy to scale if uptake is strong.

  • Improves resident access. Convenient local return points reduce reliance on individual household collections.

  • Reduces safety risk. Keeps used needles away from general waste, recycling and public spaces.

FAQs

GLP-1 Sharps Safe Return FAQs

What can go in a MediWaste sharps return bin? +

Approved sharps containers are for used needles, pen needles, lancets and similar sharp medical items. They must not be used for general rubbish, recycling, medicines, liquids or clinical waste bags.

Can residents bring GLP-1 injection pens? +

Used pen needles should always go into a sharps container. Empty pen bodies and packaging should be handled according to the manufacturer’s instructions and local guidance. MediWaste can provide clear resident-facing signage to reduce confusion.

Does this replace the council’s existing clinical-waste service? +

No. The service is designed to complement existing household clinical-waste collections. It adds convenient community return points for residents who can safely transport a sealed sharps container.

Where can return points be located? +

Suitable locations may include selected pharmacies, council offices, libraries, leisure centres, GP practices and supermarket partners. Each site is assessed for accessibility, security, footfall and operational practicality.

How often are bins collected? +

Collections can be weekly, fortnightly or based on fill levels. MediWaste can also provide an emergency call-out service if a container needs attention between scheduled visits.

Is the service compliant? +

Yes. MediWaste provides duty-of-care documentation, collection records and traceability for waste transferred through the service.

What does the council receive? +

The council receives a managed pilot with monthly reporting on collections, volumes, site performance, resident feedback and any operational issues.
